Checklist

QC photo checks for streetwear and sneakers

QC photos are not only for finding obvious flaws. They help you confirm that the item matches your expectations before you spend money on international shipping.

1. Shape and proportions

For sneakers, compare the toe shape, side profile, heel height, and sole curve. For clothing, check the cut: cropped, oversized, boxy, straight, wide-leg, or tapered. The overall shape matters more than tiny details if you plan to wear the item often.

2. Stitching and construction

Look for uneven seams, loose threads, warped panels, and obvious alignment issues. On bags and jackets, check handles, zippers, pockets, and hardware placement.

3. Fabric and texture

Photos can reveal whether a hoodie looks structured or flimsy, whether denim has the wash you expected, and whether leather-like materials look too glossy. If the texture looks wrong in warehouse lighting, it may not improve in person.

4. Color and lighting

Warehouse lighting can shift colors, so avoid overreacting to minor tone differences. Focus on large mismatches: black that reads gray, cream that reads yellow, or prints that look faded.

5. Sizing notes

If measurements are available, compare them against something you already own. For streetwear, shoulder width, length, inseam, waist, and chest measurements are more useful than generic size labels.

Review in order of decision impact

Begin with identity and variant: confirm that the photos show the ordered color, size, and product. Next check measurements and overall shape, because those can make the item unusable even when construction is clean. Then inspect damage, missing parts, asymmetry, stitching, print placement, hardware, and packaging. Mark each issue as acceptable, needs another photo, or reject. This keeps a small cosmetic flaw from distracting you from a wrong size or mismatched item.

Request one clear answer per extra photo

A useful request names the location, angle, and reference needed: “Please photograph the insole beside a ruler from heel to toe” is clearer than “more QC.” For apparel, request a flat chest, length, waist, inseam, or shoulder measurement using the method shown in the size chart. For bags, ask for the strap attachment or interior label; for shoes, ask for both heels together or a close-up of the sole edge. Save the response with the original set so the evidence stays connected.

Know what the images cannot establish

Warehouse lighting can alter color, and a still image cannot prove comfort, odor, fiber composition, waterproofing, battery safety, or long-term durability. Photos also do not guarantee that shipping will be problem-free. Use them to verify visible facts and reduce avoidable uncertainty, then consider seller terms, agent policy, landed cost, and your tolerance for a return or sizing mistake before approval.
