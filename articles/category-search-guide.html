Category guide

What to search for by category

Good search terms are specific enough to narrow results, but not so specific that you only see one seller or one version. Use the category as your anchor, then add shape, material, color, or season.

Shoes

Try terms such as low sneaker, chunky sneaker, running shoe, retro sneaker, skate shoe, or black sneaker. If the result set is too broad, add a color or silhouette.

Hoodies and sweaters

Useful terms include cropped hoodie, zip hoodie, washed hoodie, heavyweight hoodie, knit sweater, and oversized sweater. Check length and sleeve shape carefully in QC photos.

T-shirts and jerseys

Search graphic tee, heavyweight tee, boxy tee, football jersey, mesh jersey, or washed t shirt. For tees, fit and fabric weight usually matter more than the product title.

Jackets and outerwear

Start with bomber jacket, varsity jacket, denim jacket, windbreaker, puffer jacket, or work jacket. For outerwear, compare zippers, lining, collar shape, and sleeve volume.

Pants, bags, and accessories

For pants, use cargo pants, wide leg pants, denim, shorts, or track pants. For bags, search tote bag, shoulder bag, crossbody, backpack, or nylon bag. Accessories work best with direct terms like sunglasses, cap, belt, ring, or scarf.

Build a query from four useful details

Start with the category, then add only the detail that matters most: silhouette, material, color, or intended use. “Jacket” may be too broad, while “cropped nylon windbreaker” describes shape and material without depending on a copied product title. If results are weak, change one part at a time so you know which term helped. Keep synonyms in a short note—such as crossbody, shoulder bag, and messenger bag—because sellers may label the same general shape differently.

Compare within one category before switching

Choose three to five plausible options and record the same facts for each: measurements, material claim, available colors, visible construction, item price, estimated weight, and QC-photo availability. This prevents a striking hero image in one category from distracting you from a better-supported option in another. Reject entries that cannot answer a requirement you already decided was essential, such as an insole length, laptop capacity, or jacket chest width.

Let the use case break ties

Two visually similar items can serve different jobs. A daily shoe needs comfort and sizing confidence; an occasional statement piece may justify more styling effort but not missing measurements. A commuting bag needs reliable closures and capacity, while a small evening bag can prioritize proportion. Write the use case before opening listings so the comparison ends with a decision rather than another round of browsing.
