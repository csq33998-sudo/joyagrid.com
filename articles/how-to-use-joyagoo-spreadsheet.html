Tutorial

How to use Joyagoo spreadsheet without beginner mistakes

A good workflow starts with intent, not impulse. Choose a category, scan visual evidence, check QC context, estimate total cost, and save only strong candidates. The full joyagoo spreadsheet hub keeps those steps together.

Step 1: choose a category

Start with sneakers, streetwear, bags, accessories, or tech. Category-first browsing keeps the page readable and helps you compare similar items.

Step 2: inspect the product card

Use image, name, price, tags, and QC route as the first filter. Do not open every link; open only items that match your need.

Step 3: review QC photos

Look for angles that prove shape, material, stitching, hardware, color, measurements, and packaging. Ask for more photos when a detail is missing.

Step 4: estimate the real cost

Item price is only part of the decision. Add domestic shipping, service fees, international shipping, exchange buffer, and likely weight.

Step 5: verify that the row still matches the listing

A spreadsheet entry is a snapshot, not a promise that the destination remains unchanged. Confirm the seller name, product photos, selected variant, price, size options, and recent availability on the current page. If the link redirects to a different item or the useful photos have disappeared, treat the row as a discovery clue and search again. Never rely on an old title alone when colorways or versions share one listing.

Keep a short decision record

For each serious candidate, save the date checked, item and shipping estimate, intended size, key measurements, QC questions, and the reason it belongs in your wardrobe. This makes two similar options easier to compare and prevents you from forgetting which assumptions still need proof. Screenshots can preserve context, but the live product page and current agent terms should be checked again before payment.

Use stop rules before building a haul

Remove a find when the measurements cannot be confirmed, the cost range exceeds your limit, essential photos are missing, or the destination looks inconsistent with the saved entry. Pause if you are adding items only to justify shipping. A useful spreadsheet workflow reduces uncertainty; it should not turn missing information into confidence.
