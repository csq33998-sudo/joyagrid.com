Outerwear

How to search jackets and outerwear by silhouette

Outerwear changes the entire outfit, so it deserves more careful searching than a generic jacket query.

Bomber jackets

Search bomber jacket, cropped bomber, nylon bomber, or washed bomber. Check sleeve volume, hem ribbing, zipper quality, and shoulder shape.

Windbreakers

Windbreakers should be judged by panel lines, fabric shine, hood shape, and how the hem falls. Add color terms if results feel too broad.

Varsity jackets

Look closely at sleeve material, collar ribbing, snaps, patches, and overall length. The jacket should not look too stiff or too flat.

Puffers

For puffers, check fill distribution, collar height, sleeve width, and whether the body looks boxy or tapered.

Work jackets and denim jackets

Search work jacket, chore jacket, carpenter jacket, denim jacket, or washed denim jacket. Texture and pocket placement matter here.

Measure the layer, not just your body

Outerwear needs enough room for what sits underneath it. Measure a jacket you already wear with the intended base layer, then compare pit-to-pit width, shoulder width, sleeve length, back length, and hem opening. A wide chest with a narrow hem can still feel restrictive, while dropped shoulders can make the listed sleeve measurement look shorter than it wears. For puffers, remember that thick insulation reduces the usable interior space even when the outside measurements look generous.

Read construction details in the photos

Ask for a straight front view, back view, cuff and collar close-ups, pocket interiors, and a photo of the jacket closed. Check whether the zipper track lies flat, snaps align, ribbing returns to shape, and the lining is caught cleanly at the seams. On washed denim or work jackets, compare the wash across panels; extreme differences near seams can make the garment look patchy rather than naturally worn.

Choose features for the conditions

A windbreaker should have a useful hem or cuff adjustment, while a rain-oriented shell needs credible seam and fabric information rather than appearance alone. A daily commuter jacket needs pockets that close and a collar that remains comfortable with a backpack. Treat weather claims cautiously unless the listing provides material specifications or test details.
